# Evidence integrity protocol

Apply the proportionality rule: for a short, non-quantitative personal post, a brief internal fact check may suffice. For public factual claims, material business decisions, statistics, incident reports, or high-stakes topics, use the full audit.

## Claim trace (internal unless requested)

| Claim | Type | Grounding | Possible alternative explanation / qualifier | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What is proposed as true? | Observation / calculation / inference / forecast / opinion / ask | Exact source, period, measure, methodology, or first-person testimony | Missing data, uncertainty, scope, limitation | Verified / qualified / omit / needs evidence |

A first-person recollection may support an authentic anecdote without becoming independently verified fact. Do not portray it as a representative sample. For external research, record publisher, title, URL or document locator, and publication/extraction date when meaningful. Never fabricate provenance or attach a source to a claim it does not support.

## Quantitative checks

1. **Identity of metric:** numerator, denominator, population/cohort, unit, currency, geography, period, and whether actual, target, estimate, or projection.
2. **Comparability:** same definitions, time windows, sampling frames and units. State material adjustments and exceptions.
3. **Baselines:** show the relevant comparator (prior period, benchmark, target, peer, or counterfactual). Don't imply a trend from an isolated point.
4. **Changes:** distinguish absolute change from percentage change and percentage points. Recompute arithmetic when tools/data are available.
5. **Causality:** describe association or sequence unless a causal method/evidence justifies a stronger claim. Consider confounders and competing explanations.
6. **Uncertainty:** disclose uncertainty, missing values, changes in definition, small denominators, and model assumptions near affected claims. Intervals need their correct meaning; do not label a confidence interval as a guaranteed range.
7. **Selection:** examine omitted context that might reverse the interpretation; flag cherry-picked horizons, unrepresentative cases, or inconsistent baselines.
8. **Freshness and sensitivity:** verify time-sensitive facts when appropriate and authorized. Avoid revealing private, protected, or customer-specific data in public communication.

## Controlled language ladder

| Evidence status | Suitable formulation | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Directly measured | "The measured value was ..." | "This proves ..." without justification |
| Correlational | "X was associated with Y" | "X caused Y" |
| Modelled scenario | "Under assumptions A and B, the model suggests ..." | "Y will happen" |
| Incomplete | "Available information is consistent with ...; we have not verified ..." | A definitive success/failure claim |
| Opinion / recommendation | "I recommend X because ..." | Presenting a preference as observed fact |

## Evidence gates

**Hard stop for the problematic claim:** invented source, unsupported metric, materially misleading comparator, causal language unsupported by evidence, fabricated personal experience, or a known contradictory source hidden without disclosure. Do not suppress the entire deliverable if a faithful version is possible: narrow, label, substitute a supported claim, or leave a visible question/placeholder.

For persuasive content, be able to steelman the strongest relevant objection and distinguish it from irrelevant objections; don't overstate consensus. The audience may want persuasion, but data integrity still comes first.
